ADUs & Home Additions

San Diego ADU Feasibility: Site, Utilities & Access

A floor plan that fits in the backyard is only the beginning of ADU feasibility. Before choosing finishes, establish where the unit can go, how it will connect to services and how it can be built without overlooking the existing home. Those checks help distinguish a promising concept from a complete project budget.

Confirm the authority and the type of unit

Start with the parcel address and identify the responsible city or County department. For the City of San Diego, City of San Diego: ADU and JADU Requirements (IB 400) covers ADUs, JADUs and permit requirements, and warns that coastal rules differ. Ask staff to classify your proposed detached unit, attached addition or conversion before relying on a generic ADU checklist.

Record the intended use and occupants, the existing dwelling configuration and any known easements. Avoid assuming that a preapproved floor plan establishes approval for the site; location, utility and site-specific documents still need attention.

Draw the site beyond the new walls

A useful concept plan shows the existing house, proposed unit, property boundaries, access route, major trees, slopes and known utilities. Add drainage direction and the outdoor space you want to keep. Ask the designer which boundaries or elevations need a survey before the layout becomes detailed.

Compare two placements if the lot permits it. A slightly different orientation might improve privacy or shorten a utility route. Evaluate that tradeoff alongside bedroom size rather than choosing a layout solely by its advertised square footage.

Establish a utility plan early

Ask a qualified electrician or plumber to identify the likely connection points and the information needed for service planning. Do not assume the existing panel has spare capacity or that a sewer line follows the shortest visible path. List what has been verified and what requires locating, calculations or further investigation.

SDG&E asks applicants to gather project documentation and appliance-load information. Its service application guidance also says an ADU address must be established through the municipality before applying. Confirm the current requirements directly with the utility. SDG&E: Apply for Service.

Keep utility-provider work, private trenching, equipment and pavement restoration as separate estimate items. That makes exclusions visible without pretending the utility’s final scope is already known.

Walk the equipment and delivery route

Measure access at gates, corners and overhead obstructions. Identify where excavated soil, materials and debris can wait. If equipment cannot use the proposed route, ask how the work will be done and how the pricing changes.

Consider daily access to the existing home, pets, laundry and parking during construction. Put fence removal and replacement, landscape repairs and temporary protection into the scope. These tasks are easy to miss when the estimate starts and ends at the new building’s walls.

Make a feasibility decision before a finish decision

  • Document the jurisdiction’s response to the proposed unit and location.
  • Identify surveys, design work and investigations still needed.
  • List utility assumptions and the next person responsible for resolving each one.
  • Compare the building price with a total that includes site work, fees and restoration.
  • Choose the next design stage only after those costs and uncertainties fit your budget.

Questions homeowners ask

Does a small ADU automatically have a simple utility connection?

No. Size is only one input. Existing service capacity, connection locations and the site route can matter as much as the unit’s floor area.

Should I choose a detached ADU or convert the garage?

Compare both against the property. A garage offers an existing enclosure, but its slab, structure and conversion requirements may change the economics. A detached unit can offer different privacy and layout options while adding new site work.
