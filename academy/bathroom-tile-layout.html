Choosing and Installing Tile: Layout, Grout & Wet-Area Details

Tile selection is only one part of a durable installation. The surface underneath, water-management details, movement joints and layout all affect the result. Settle those decisions before comparing tile-setting prices or ordering the full quantity.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Match the tile to its intended location
  • Approve the layout where cuts will be visible
  • Price the preparation and wet-area assembly
  • Plan grout and movement joints separately
  • Treat outdoor tile as a distinct assembly
  • Keep installation and care records

Match the tile to its intended location

Porcelain is a category of ceramic tile defined by low water absorption; the name alone does not establish suitability for every floor or outdoor condition. TCNA advises checking manufacturer information for the intended use. Tile Council of North America: Porcelain and Non-Porcelain Tile.

Ask for the product’s floor, wall and exterior application guidance, including wet-use information where relevant. Evaluate texture and cleaning with a sample. No tile label removes the need to manage water, slopes and maintenance.

Approve the layout where cuts will be visible

Draw the major joints, focal walls, niches, drains and transitions. Check the pattern at doorways and around fixtures rather than centering each wall independently. A tile that looks balanced on one wall can create a narrow cut at the next corner.

Discuss variation and blending before installation. Keep samples or photos of the agreed arrangement, including grout color and joint appearance. Confirm the order quantity with the installer after the pattern and cuts are established.

Price the preparation and wet-area assembly

Ask the installer to assess the substrate and specify the preparation needed for the chosen tile. Large pieces make surface irregularities especially important to resolve. Include removal, repair and transitions in the quote rather than assuming the surface will be ready.

In a shower, the waterproofing and drain connection require their own assembly and verification. A tile-setting price or a grout sealer does not define that work. Keep the inspection and testing sequence ahead of concealment.

Plan grout and movement joints separately

Grout fills the tile joints; it does not replace the flexible accommodation needed for movement. TCNA explains that movement-joint placement depends on site conditions, including sunlight and moisture exposure. Ask the installer to identify the layout and appropriate materials. Tile Council of North America: Movement Joint Placement.

Select grout compatible with the tile and joint design, using the product instructions for installation and cure. Ask whether the specified grout needs sealing and how it should be cleaned. Tile Council of North America: Grout.

Treat outdoor tile as a distinct assembly

For a patio or exterior landing, evaluate drainage, exposure, substrate and transitions at the building. Do not carry an interior tile specification outdoors just to maintain the same appearance. Ask how water reaches an approved discharge point and how movement is accommodated.

Compare the finished threshold height and access needs with the drainage design. A visually flush transition should not be achieved by ignoring weather protection at the door.

Keep installation and care records

Retain product names, grout and sealant information, spare material and approved cleaning instructions. Observe the specified cure and return-to-use times. If joints crack or tiles become loose, have the condition assessed before covering the symptoms with new grout.

Questions homeowners ask

Is porcelain automatically the best tile for a shower floor?

Not necessarily. Check the exact product’s intended use, texture, dimensions and compatibility with the drain and floor slopes.

Can I eliminate grout joints for a seamless look?

Do not assume that a tight visual joint can replace the installation requirements. Have the installer establish appropriate joints and movement accommodation for the chosen product.
