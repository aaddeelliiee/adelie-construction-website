Bathroom Remodeling

Bathroom Layout: Vanities, Storage, Lighting & Bathtubs

A bathroom can have attractive fixtures and still be frustrating to use. Plan the vanity, storage, lighting and bathing area as one room. The useful test is whether doors, drawers, people and maintenance access can coexist in the space available.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide

  • Coordinate the sink and plumbing before cabinet approval

Start with movement and storage needs

Measure the room and draw door swings, drawers and the approach to each fixture. List what actually needs storage: towels, cleaning products, toiletries and electrical grooming items. Decide what should be reachable daily and what can live elsewhere.

A wider vanity can reduce circulation without adding much useful storage if plumbing occupies its center. Compare drawer arrangements and access around the sink before treating overall width as the only measure of value.

Coordinate the sink and plumbing before cabinet approval

Choose the sink type and faucet arrangement with the vanity drawing. Check the sink bowl, drain, supply connections and drawer boxes together. A wall-mounted faucet requires coordination inside the wall; it cannot be treated as a finish selection to settle after tile.

If fixtures move, have the plumber and designer establish the route and restoration work. Keep service access and shutoffs usable. Include countertop holes and accessory locations in the approved package.

Use light where the face and floor need it

Evaluate mirror lighting with the user standing at the sink, and include general light and nighttime movement. Avoid choosing fixtures solely from their appearance above an empty vanity. Confirm suitability for the proposed wet or damp location and compatibility with controls.

California’s lighting guidance covers alterations as well as new work. Have the electrician confirm the requirements for the chosen fixtures and controls. California Energy Commission: 2025 Residential Lighting Questions.

Choose a tub by access and maintenance as well as shape

Compare alcove, freestanding and other tub arrangements using the actual product drawings. Check the space to get in and out, the reach to controls, the route for installation and access for service. A freestanding tub placed too close to a wall can leave an awkward cleaning gap.

Have the project team evaluate support, water supply and drainage needs for the selected tub. Consider whether the household will use it often enough to justify the floor area compared with a larger shower or more storage.

Approve one coordinated bathroom drawing

  • Vanity, sink and faucet specifications with plumbing clearances.
  • Drawer and door operation, including adjacent room doors.
  • Mirror, fixture and control locations.
  • Tub installation requirements and service access.
  • Storage that does not interfere with waterproofing or structure.

Use a full-size mockup or taped layout where space is tight. Resolve the conflicts before ordering rather than asking the installer to make the products fit after delivery.

Questions homeowners ask

Are two sinks always better than one?

Not if they remove the counter and storage your household needs. Compare how two people use the room at the same time with the space each arrangement leaves.

Can I recess a medicine cabinet wherever I want?

Have the wall checked for structure, wiring and plumbing first. A recess needs a suitable location and coordination with the mirror and lighting.
