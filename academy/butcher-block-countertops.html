Wood Countertops: Finish, Sink Details & Maintenance

The finish on a wood countertop changes how you use and maintain it. Decide whether the surface is intended for direct food preparation, general counter use or a decorative accent before choosing the product. A cutting board routine is not automatically the right routine for every finished wood counter.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide

  • Review water exposure at the sink

Choose the use and finish together

John Boos publishes separate care guidance for oil-finished and Varnique-finished countertops and worktops. That distinction is a useful reminder to identify the actual finish before selecting cleaners or treatments. Follow the instructions for the product you buy, rather than applying one brand’s routine to all wood. John Boos: Countertop and Worktop Care.

Ask whether direct cutting is intended, how heat should be managed and what contact with water the installation is designed to accommodate. Keep the written answers with the order.

Review water exposure at the sink

A sink opening, faucet hole and wall junction all need coordination with the selected countertop system. Ask the fabricator to specify the cutout treatment and explain how exposed edges and joints will be protected. Include the faucet and sink model before fabrication.

Consider whether the household is willing to manage standing water and keep the finish maintained. If the sink is heavily used, compare a different surface in that area with wood used elsewhere in the kitchen.

Allow for the material and its installation

Have the fabricator identify support, attachment and movement requirements for the chosen construction. Do not assume a solid-looking top can be fastened like a stone slab. Coordinate adjoining walls, appliances and changes of material with the installer.

Confirm the supplied dimensions, edge treatment and finish on all relevant surfaces. If field cutting is needed, establish who restores the specified finish before the counter goes into use.

Keep maintenance practical

Request a written routine that names the approved products and the signs that attention is needed. Keep that routine separate from ordinary cleaning. Discuss what a scratch, dry area or water mark should prompt you to do and who can assess a problem.

A heavily used kitchen needs a routine the household will actually follow. If ongoing finish care is unwelcome, compare other countertop options before choosing wood solely for its initial appearance.

Inspect the completed installation

  • Confirm the product and finish match the order.
  • Check joints, cutouts and accessible edges with the installer.
  • Verify that the surface is ready for use under its finish instructions.
  • Collect care products or specifications and warranty terms.
  • Record the contact for repairs or refinishing.

Keep a separate cutting board available unless the selected surface is specifically intended and maintained for direct cutting.

Questions homeowners ask

Should every wood countertop be oiled?

No. Identify the finish first. A film-finished countertop and an oil-finished work surface may require different treatment.

Can wood work next to a sink?

Evaluate the selected product, cutout details and maintenance expectations with the fabricator. Do not assume that any unfinished edge or ordinary furniture finish is suitable.

Sources and local requirements

References checked October 24, 2026. City of San Diego guidance applies within that city; other cities and unincorporated areas have their own authorities. Product instructions apply to the specified system.
