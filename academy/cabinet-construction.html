Kitchen Remodeling

Choosing Kitchen Cabinets: Construction, Style & Installation

Two cabinet quotes can look similar on a floor plan while delivering very different kitchens. Compare the actual cabinet specification and installation scope before deciding that stock, semi-custom or custom is the best value. The label alone does not tell you how well the cabinets will fit the room or serve your household.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below

Compare the specification behind the door

Ask for the box material, back construction, drawer construction, hinges, slides and finish system. Open a sample drawer, inspect its movement and ask how replacement hardware is obtained. Compare the same features across suppliers rather than treating one material name as a complete quality rating.

KCMA’s certification tests address cabinet structure, door and drawer operation, and finish performance. A verified certification can provide useful evidence about a product; it does not replace checking your order or the installation. KCMA: Cabinet Certification and Performance Tests.

Choose style with the whole room in view

Bring a door sample into the room and examine it in daylight and under the proposed lighting. Compare the profile, finish and visible joints with the flooring and counter. Ask to see how the finish looks on an end panel, not just a small color chip.

Shaker, slab and inset describe different design choices, not a ranking from inexpensive to expensive. Ask the supplier to explain how the selected construction affects gaps, adjustment, cleaning and available storage. Confirm which visible ends receive finished panels and which interiors will be seen through glass.

Make sizing flexibility earn its cost

Stock sizes may fit the room well, while a difficult corner or unusual depth may benefit from more customization. Compare drawings showing the usable result: drawer widths, corner access, tray storage and the space beside appliances. An expensive cabinet that stores less is not automatically an upgrade.

Use actual appliance model numbers and installation sheets. Resolve fillers, door swings and access to appliance service points before approving fabrication. Panel-ready appliances need coordination among the appliance supplier, cabinet supplier and installer.

Put hardware and installation in the price

Specify pull and knob locations before drilling. Test whether adjacent handles collide and whether a large appliance handle interferes with a drawer. Ask who supplies hardware, templates the holes and replaces a damaged or incorrectly drilled front.

The installation scope should address delivery, receiving inspection, protection, layout, leveling, fastening, fillers, trim and adjustments. Identify who prepares walls and floors and who repairs surrounding finishes. A cabinet-only price cannot be compared fairly with an installed price.

Approve the drawings, then inspect the delivery

  • Check overall dimensions, appliance openings and cabinet-by-cabinet storage.
  • Confirm finished sides, panels, fillers, trim and hardware.
  • Record finish and door-style approval using a retained sample.
  • Confirm installation responsibilities and the process for shortages or damage.
  • Keep the approved drawing revision and inspect components before installation.

Walk the finished kitchen with doors and drawers open. Check alignment, access, clearances and remaining adjustments before signing off on the cabinet portion of the job.

Questions homeowners ask

Are custom cabinets always better than stock cabinets?

No. Custom sizing can solve a difficult layout, but compare the construction, finish, storage and installation of the actual products. A well-specified stock kitchen may serve the household better than an unnecessarily complicated custom layout.

Should I buy the cabinets before hiring the installer?

Coordinate the measured layout and installation requirements first. Otherwise, field conditions or missing panels can create changes after the order is difficult to revise.
