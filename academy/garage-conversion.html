Garage Conversion in San Diego: What Must Be Checked?

Keeping the garage walls does not necessarily make a conversion inexpensive. The important question is how much of the existing building can serve the proposed living space. Evaluate that before treating the garage’s square footage as ready-made housing.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide

  • Investigate the floor before drawing the bathroom
  • Evaluate the enclosure as a whole
  • Put services and the surrounding property in the estimate

Decide what the converted space will be

A room attached to the main home, an independent ADU and a JADU are different proposals. Describe the kitchen, bathroom, access and intended arrangement to the local department. Inside the City of San Diego, use City of San Diego: ADU and JADU Requirements (IB 400) to identify the relevant application path. Parking and coastal provisions need a location-specific check; do not promise that every conversion has the same parking exemption.

Check the existing garage’s records and actual dimensions. If it has already been altered, establish which parts were approved before designing around them.

Investigate the floor before drawing the bathroom

Have the design team evaluate slab condition, levels and the proposed finish height. Work out how the bathroom drain will connect and what excavation or other construction the route requires. A neat bathroom arrangement on a plan can become difficult if it ignores the existing floor or connection depth.

Include moisture evaluation and a floor-system specification appropriate to the conditions. Covering an uncertain slab with a new finish is not the same as resolving its suitability for occupied space.

Evaluate the enclosure as a whole

Ask which existing walls, roof members and foundations can remain and what verification supports that decision. Coordinate insulation, windows, doors, weather protection and heating or cooling with the proposed use. Replacing the garage door with a wall is one part of that work, not a complete conversion scope.

A retained roof or wall can save work, but repairing around it may limit the layout. Compare retaining it with rebuilding the affected portion before assuming reuse is always the cheaper option.

Put services and the surrounding property in the estimate

Locate the proposed electrical, water and wastewater connections. Identify whether separate utility coordination is needed and which costs remain provisional. Include trenching, driveway repairs and any relocated storage or equipment.

Think through what the garage currently does. If it contains laundry, a water heater, household storage or the main access route, decide where those functions will go. The replacement arrangements are part of the project even though they may sit outside the new dwelling.

Compare like-for-like options

Ask for a scope comparison between conversion and a new detached unit with similar usable rooms and finishes. List retained construction, corrective work, site work and outstanding investigations separately. A conversion quote that excludes service work cannot be compared directly with a detached-unit quote that includes it.

There is no responsible universal conversion discount. Use the property findings to decide whether the existing garage is an advantage, a constraint or a mixture of both.

Questions homeowners ask

Can I move into the converted garage once the finishes are done?

Confirm the required final approvals with the permitting authority before using it as living space. Completed flooring and appliances do not establish that the permitted work has been accepted.

Will a conversion always be faster than new construction?

Not necessarily. Existing conditions, corrections and utility coordination can affect either option. Ask for a sequence tied to the actual approvals and investigations.
