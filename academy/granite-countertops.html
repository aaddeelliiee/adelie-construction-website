Granite Countertops: Slab Selection, Sealing & Care

Choose granite by looking at the slabs you are buying, not just the trade name on a small sample. Natural variation is part of the material. The best time to decide how that variation appears in your kitchen is before the slabs are cut.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Approve the stone and the important cuts
  • Resolve edges, support and access
  • Get a stone-specific sealing recommendation
  • Write the care plan around actual use
  • Compare the installed scope

Approve the stone and the important cuts

Ask the supplier to identify the exact slabs reserved for your project and view them under useful lighting. Discuss variation, filled areas and any features that need explanation from the fabricator. Photograph the approved slabs and identify the areas you want to emphasize or avoid.

Review the proposed layout around the sink, range and island. Two pieces from attractive slabs can still create a seam you dislike. Approve the relationship of the pieces, not only their individual appearance.

Resolve edges, support and access

Confirm the edge profile, thickness, cutouts and support arrangement with the fabricator. Review cabinet readiness before templating and identify who corrects an unsuitable base. A stone counter should not be used to disguise an unresolved cabinet problem.

Measure the delivery route and discuss how large pieces will enter the home. In a condo, coordinate elevator access and building rules. Include protection and restoration of the delivery path in the work plan.

Get a stone-specific sealing recommendation

The Natural Stone Institute explains that not all stone needs sealing and that an impregnating sealer improves stain resistance rather than making a surface stain-proof. Ask the supplier whether your stone needs treatment, which product is appropriate and how its performance should be maintained. Natural Stone Institute: Care and Cleaning.

A yearly sealing routine is not a useful universal rule. Keep the product and application record, including any treatment already applied by the supplier. Do not add a coating simply because an unrelated online checklist recommends it.

Write the care plan around actual use

Use the supplier’s approved cleaning method and address spills promptly. Ask which cleaners should be avoided and what to do about an oil stain, chip or mark near a seam. Keep aggressive household cleaners away from the surface unless the manufacturer or stone-care professional confirms they are suitable.

Use cutting boards and appropriate protection for hot cookware. These habits help protect the installation and surrounding finishes without relying on broad claims that natural stone is indestructible.

Compare the installed scope

Request pricing for slabs, layout, fabrication, edge work, sink cutouts, supports, transport and installation. Identify demolition and plumbing reconnection separately. If one price uses fewer seams or more carefully matched pieces, ask how that affects slab quantity and cost.

At the final walkthrough, review seams, edges, fixture fit and the care documents with the installer. Keep supplier and installer contacts together so a later issue can be directed to the right person.

Questions homeowners ask

Is granite automatically stain-proof after sealing?

No. A sealer can improve stain resistance, but it does not eliminate the need for appropriate cleaning and spill management. Follow the recommendations for the actual stone and treatment.

Can I choose from a sample without seeing the slab?

You can, but you accept more uncertainty about the finished pattern. Seeing and approving the actual slabs and layout is especially valuable when natural variation matters to you.
