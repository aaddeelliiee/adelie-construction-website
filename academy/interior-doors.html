Materials & Finishes

Choosing Interior Doors, Trim and Finish Details Together

A door selection is more than a panel style. The opening, swing, hardware and surrounding trim all affect whether it works comfortably and looks intentional. Make those decisions together, particularly when new work meets existing rooms.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Choose construction for the room’s needs
  • Check the opening and the swing
  • Coordinate the jamb, casing and baseboard
  • Make one finish schedule for connected rooms
  • Check operation at handover

Choose construction for the room’s needs

Hollow-core, solid-core and solid-wood doors are different constructions. The Masonite: Interior Door Types is a useful starting point for comparing them. Ask the supplier about the exact product’s weight, finish options and intended use.

For a bedroom or office, discuss privacy and sound expectations. A heavier panel alone does not make a room soundproof: the frame, gaps and surrounding construction matter too. For a utility space, confirm any ventilation or other assembly requirements before changing the door.

Check the opening and the swing

Measure the existing opening and wall thickness, and confirm whether the proposal replaces just the slab or the frame as well. Review the swing against furniture, switches, adjacent doors and bathroom fixtures.

A pocket door may save swing space, but the wall needs evaluation for structure, services and hardware access. Show the intended clear opening on the plan rather than relying only on a nominal door size.

Coordinate the jamb, casing and baseboard

Set the relationship between casing and baseboard before ordering. Review thickness, profiles and how trim ends at cabinets, tile or built-in furniture. Existing walls may need preparation to achieve the desired result.

Check floor height changes and transitions between rooms. Ask who adjusts doors and trim after flooring is installed. A simple detail at the showroom can become awkward where an old wall is uneven or the new floor is higher.

Make one finish schedule for connected rooms

For every opening, record the door model, size, swing, frame, casing, hardware finish and paint or stain. Add the baseboard profile and nearby floor transition. Choose one representative junction to review before repeating it throughout the house.

Matching does not require every material to be identical. For example, painted doors can work with a different cabinet finish if the trim and hardware choices are deliberate. Compare real samples together rather than trusting similar names across brands.

Check operation at handover

Open, close and latch each door. Review clearances, alignment, hardware and visible finish repairs. Obtain care instructions and replacement hardware information, and record any adjustments still needed.

If a door’s function is part of a required fire, ventilation or accessibility arrangement, have the project professional confirm the completed assembly. Do not substitute components based on appearance alone.

Questions homeowners ask

Can I replace only the door panel?

Sometimes. The existing frame’s dimensions and condition, hinge positions and new panel compatibility need checking before ordering.

Should baseboards be selected after the floor?

Coordinate them together, including thickness, transitions and installation sequence. Waiting can leave awkward gaps or trim details.
