Kitchen Remodeling

Planning Kitchen and Outdoor Lighting That Works After Dark

A ceiling full of lights can still leave the cutting board in shadow. Start with the surfaces you use, then decide where the light should come from. Decorative fixtures work better when they are part of that plan.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Draw the work surfaces first
  • Keep a fixture and control schedule
  • Coordinate the installation before walls close
  • Treat exterior lighting as its own set of zones
  • Review the result at night

Draw the work surfaces first

Mark the sink, preparation counter, range, island seating and main walking routes on the cabinet plan. Think through an ordinary evening: unloading groceries, washing dishes and leaving a low light on after dinner. Each activity may need a different lighting setting.

Coordinate ceiling fixtures with cabinet fronts and the position of the person working. Under-cabinet lighting can help illuminate a counter, but its location and diffuser affect reflections on polished stone. Review an actual fixture sample where possible.

Keep a fixture and control schedule

For each light, record its model, location, finish, light output, color temperature, dimmer compatibility and who supplies it. Check replacement access and the appearance of nearby fixtures together. A warm pendant and a much cooler counter light can make the same cabinet finish look inconsistent.

Group controls around use: preparation lights, dining or island lights, and general circulation. Ask the electrician to confirm the proposed combinations before ordering. Dimmable on a product box does not confirm compatibility with every dimmer.

Coordinate the installation before walls close

Locate drivers, wiring routes and accessible service points before cabinetry and drywall are finished. Confirm mounting support for the selected fixtures and identify who patches openings. California lighting and control requirements depend on the work; use the California Energy Commission: 2025 Residential Lighting Questions with the project professional.

Walk the marked layout with the cabinet drawings in hand. An island moved during design can leave a previously centered junction box in the wrong place.

Treat exterior lighting as its own set of zones

Outside, separate the entry, steps, dining area and landscape accents. Begin with safe circulation and useful light, then add decorative accents. Aim fixtures to limit glare into seating areas and neighboring windows; consider how planting will grow around them.

Select equipment for its actual outdoor exposure and have the electrician define supply, protection and controls. Do not extend an indoor fixture schedule into a wet location by assumption. Keep access to drivers and connections practical for maintenance.

Review the result at night

At handover, try each control and dimming range. Look for reflections from a seated position, shadows on the working counter and glare along outdoor paths. Record any adjustments before the final punch list closes, and save the fixture schedule for future replacements.

Questions homeowners ask

Should all the lights have the same color temperature?

A consistent starting point often helps connected rooms, but evaluate the actual products and finishes together. Deliberate differences should be tested rather than discovered after installation.

Can lighting be decided after cabinets are installed?

Some selections can change later, but wiring, service access and integrated cabinet lights are easier to coordinate before installation.

Sources and local requirements

References checked October 24, 2026. City of San Diego guidance applies within that city; other cities and unincorporated areas have their own authorities. Product instructions apply to the specified system.
