Kitchen Remodeling

Kitchen Range Hood Planning: Capture, Ducts & California Review

A range hood affects cabinet dimensions, the route through the house and how comfortable the kitchen is to use. Choose it alongside the cooking appliance, not after the cabinet design is finished. The visible hood is only one part of the exhaust system.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Begin with the cooking arrangement
  • Trace the duct before approving the cabinets
  • Check the applicable California review
  • Discuss noise and replacement air
  • Finish with an operating handover

Begin with the cooking arrangement

Confirm the cooking appliance model, location and installation requirements. Review the hood’s intended coverage and mounting arrangement with the designer and installer. EPA recommends using a hood that exhausts outdoors where available, using it while cooking and maintaining the grease filter. EPA: Improving Indoor Air Quality While Cooking.

Do not treat a decorative enclosure or a recirculating unit as proof that the proposed ventilation meets the project’s requirements. Ask the responsible professional to explain the selected approach.

Trace the duct before approving the cabinets

Draw the proposed path to the exterior, including changes in direction, structural obstacles and the termination. Check what space remains for cabinets, ceiling details and service access. Have the installer follow the equipment’s duct requirements rather than choosing a smaller route just because it fits a decorative cover.

An island cooking location can raise different routing questions from a range against an exterior wall. Compare the complete work before deciding to move the cooking area. Retaining a workable location may simplify the remodel without compromising the household’s main goals.

Check the applicable California review

The California Energy Commission’s 2025 single-family HVAC guidance distinguishes a vented-hood replacement using existing ducts from a new hood with an entirely new duct system for field-verification purposes. That distinction can affect the project paperwork and sequence. Have the designer confirm the applicable code edition, building type and scope. California Energy Commission: 2025 Single-Family HVAC Questions.

A single-family FAQ should not be used as a blanket rule for a multifamily condo. Identify the property type and responsible jurisdiction before relying on an exemption.

Discuss noise and replacement air

Compare usable operating settings and sound information, not only maximum airflow. A system that the household avoids because it is too loud is a poor outcome. Ask how the proposed exhaust interacts with the home and whether replacement-air provisions or other measures are required.

Have a qualified professional assess those questions, particularly where fuel-burning equipment is present. A higher advertised airflow is not automatically a better or complete design.

Finish with an operating handover

  • Confirm the model, duct route and accessible service points.
  • Retain required inspection or verification records.
  • Learn the controls and the filter-removal method.
  • Obtain cleaning instructions for the hood and decorative surround.
  • Identify the contact for unexpected noise, odors or poor capture.

Include exterior repairs and cabinet finishing in the installation scope so the ventilation work is complete both inside and outside the kitchen.

Questions homeowners ask

Can I select the hood after ordering cabinets?

That can force revisions to openings, heights or duct routing. Coordinate the hood and cooking appliance before releasing the cabinet drawings.

Does every hood replacement require the same verification?

No. California’s published guidance distinguishes certain alteration scopes. Ask the project team to document which requirements apply to your specific installation and building type.
