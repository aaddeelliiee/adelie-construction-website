LVP, Engineered Wood or Solid Wood? A Remodel Comparison

Choose flooring for the room and the floor beneath it, not only for the sample board. LVP, engineered wood and solid wood offer different appearance, maintenance and repair options. A useful comparison includes the preparation and transitions needed to install each one in your home.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Understand what you are comparing
  • Investigate the existing floor first
  • Compare the room-to-room result
  • Think about repair as well as durability
  • Choose care routines you can maintain

Understand what you are comparing

Solid wood is wood through its thickness. Engineered wood has a real wood surface with a layered construction; it is not the same category as a vinyl plank printed to resemble wood. NWFA explains these distinctions in its NWFA: Solid and Engineered Wood Flooring.

Compare exact products rather than assuming all engineered floors or all LVP perform alike. Request the installation method, approved locations, care instructions and warranty exclusions. For wood, ask whether and how the selected product can be refinished; the answer depends on its construction and condition.

Investigate the existing floor first

Have the installer assess the substrate, elevations and moisture conditions before final pricing. As one product-specific example, Shaw’s STA-TITE vinyl instructions require moisture testing of substrates receiving moisture-sensitive floor covering. Use the instructions for your selected product rather than transferring that document’s details to every vinyl floor. Shaw: STA-TITE Vinyl Plank and Tile Installation Guidelines.

A waterproof-product claim does not establish that a damp slab, leak or damaged subfloor can be ignored. Identify the cause of a moisture problem and obtain an appropriate preparation plan before covering it.

Compare the room-to-room result

Draw transitions to tile, stairs, exterior doors and rooms where flooring will remain. Check finished heights at appliances and door bottoms. In a kitchen, establish the installation sequence around cabinets and follow the selected flooring system’s requirements.

For an upstairs condo, obtain the association’s sound-control requirements before selecting an underlayment. A quiet showroom sample does not prove that the complete installed assembly meets the building’s rules.

Think about repair as well as durability

Ask how a damaged board would be replaced in the proposed installation and what matching material should be retained. For wood, distinguish recoating the finish from sanding and refinishing the floor. For LVP, ask whether access to a damaged plank requires removing adjacent flooring or another approved repair method.

Evaluate texture, glare and visible pattern repetition using several boards laid out together. If a kitchen and living room will share one surface, test the appearance under both lighting conditions.

Choose care routines you can maintain

NWFA recommends prompt spill cleanup and appropriate finish-specific cleaning, and advises against wet or steam mopping wood floors. Follow the selected floor’s care instructions. NWFA: Wood Floor Maintenance.

For LVP, obtain the manufacturer’s approved cleaning and protection guidance rather than assuming that every vinyl product tolerates the same chemicals, heat or equipment. Keep furniture-protection and replacement-material requirements in the handover record.

Compare total installed prices including removal, preparation, transitions, trim and appliance handling. A lower material price can be outweighed by additional preparation or a less suitable result.

Questions homeowners ask

Is engineered wood the same as laminate or vinyl?

No. Engineered wood includes a real wood surface. Compare the exact construction and maintenance requirements rather than relying on a wood-look label.

Is LVP always the best choice for a kitchen?

Not automatically. Consider the product’s approved use, substrate, installation sequence, appliance access, appearance and repair options. The right choice depends on the room and the household.
