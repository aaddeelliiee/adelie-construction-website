San Diego Planning Considerations

Remodeling an Older San Diego Home: Investigate Before Pricing

An older home can have several generations of work hidden behind one finished wall. The useful question is not simply how old the house is. It is which parts of the existing construction your new design will rely on, and what needs to be verified before you commit to that design.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below

Start with a measured plan and a short uncertainty list

Gather original drawings, later permit records and photos of previous renovations. Compare them with the rooms as they exist. Record uneven floors, recurring stains, inaccessible crawl spaces and unexplained changes in ceiling height without jumping to a diagnosis.

Ask the project team to separate known work from investigation: for example, replacing a visible fixture is a defined task; deciding whether an old concealed pipe can remain may need access and evaluation. That distinction makes estimates easier to compare.

Check historic review before changing exterior details

For property in the City of San Diego, age can affect the review route even when a house has no historic plaque. The City’s guidance addresses work on buildings 45 years old or older, with scope-dependent review and exceptions. Check the parcel and proposal with staff before replacing windows or removing original exterior features. City of San Diego: Work on Buildings 45 Years Old or Older.

Other local cities have their own processes. Preserve photographs and dimensions of trim, doors, windows and roof details you hope to retain. Ask whether repair, replication or replacement fits both the project and the applicable review.

Plan investigation without creating an exposure problem

EPA provides specific lead-safe renovation guidance for pre-1978 housing. Before disturbing painted surfaces in a home of that age, discuss the applicable assessment and work practices with a qualified renovation professional. EPA: Lead-Safe Renovation, Repair and Painting.

EPA also cautions against disturbing suspect asbestos-containing materials without appropriate professional evaluation. Do not use a casual demolition opening to test what a material is. Include any needed evaluation and controlled work in the plan before ordinary demolition starts. EPA: Indoor Air Quality During Remodeling.

Coordinate the systems you will uncover

Use the proposed layout to ask focused questions: can the electrical system support the selected appliances, where will a relocated drain run, and can heating or cooling reach the altered room? Have the relevant professionals evaluate the systems rather than assuming that every older component must be replaced—or that every working component can stay.

Schedule agreed investigations before long-lead orders where possible. If a new beam or duct route will lower the ceiling, the cabinet design needs that information before fabrication. Show restoration of opened walls and adjoining finishes in the estimate.

Preserve character with a room-by-room decision list

Label existing features “retain,” “repair,” “match” or “replace.” Include photos of details that matter to you, such as a built-in cabinet or the transition between original flooring and an addition. A clear list prevents salvageable material from disappearing during demolition.

For the budget, keep a reserve tied to unresolved conditions and identify when each unknown should become a priced decision. Use the findings to adjust scope before spending the reserve on finish upgrades.

Questions homeowners ask

Does an older home automatically need a complete rewire?

Age alone cannot answer that. Have the electrical system assessed against its condition, proposed loads and the work being permitted. The estimate should explain the basis for keeping or replacing components.

Can I keep original windows and still remodel the interior?

Possibly. The interior scope, condition of the windows and applicable historic or energy requirements matter. Show which windows remain and which change so the designer and reviewing authority can evaluate the proposal accurately.
