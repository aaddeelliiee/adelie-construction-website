Contractors & Contracts

California Remodel Payment Schedules and Change Orders

A payment schedule should let you see what a payment covers. A change order should let you see what changed. When either document is vague, it becomes harder to compare the project’s progress with the amount you have paid.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Check the deposit rule before paying
  • Tie progress payments to identifiable work
  • Approve changes before the changed work begins
  • Keep one running financial record
  • Resolve discrepancies while they are small

Check the deposit rule before paying

For California home improvement contracts, CSLB states that the down payment generally cannot exceed $1,000 or 10% of the contract price, whichever is less. A rare exception applies to contractors with a qualifying blanket performance and payment bond on file with CSLB. An ordinary contractor license bond is not that exception. Verify any claimed exception through CSLB. CSLB: Contracts and Binding Agreements.

Do not confuse the deposit with a later progress payment. Ask for each amount and its purpose in writing before transferring money.

Tie progress payments to identifiable work

Use descriptions you can compare with the site and contract: the specified cabinets delivered, or the defined installation phase completed, rather than simply “payment three.” The milestone also needs an amount and a clear description of the work or materials it covers.

CSLB states that payments cannot exceed the value of work performed, apart from the permitted down payment. Review the proposed schedule against the contract’s scope and CSLB’s requirements. Ask what evidence supports each invoice and how partially completed milestones are handled. A calendar date alone does not explain what has been provided.

Approve changes before the changed work begins

Use a written change order signed by both parties before proceeding with changed work. Identify the reason, added and removed work, price adjustment and schedule effect. CSLB’s CSLB: Home Improvement Contract Requirements describes the requirements.

For example, replacing a selected vanity should show the credit for the original item, the replacement cost and any plumbing or delivery effect. “Upgrade vanity” leaves too much unresolved. If the price is not yet known, establish the investigation or pricing step instead of treating an unanswered message as approval.

Keep one running financial record

Track the original contract amount, each approved addition or credit, the revised total, payments made and the remaining balance. Keep invoices, receipts and signed changes with that record. Compare it with the contractor’s statement regularly.

An allowance reconciliation should explain what the allowance covered and how the actual selection changes that amount. Ask whether delivery, installation and taxes are already included before assuming the entire difference is an upgrade charge.

Resolve discrepancies while they are small

If an invoice does not match the agreed milestone, ask for the supporting detail and discuss the discrepancy promptly. Keep the conversation and any resolution documented. Obtain professional advice for contract disputes or lien questions rather than relying on a generic internet rule about withholding payment.

Before closeout, reconcile approved changes and remaining work against the contract. Keep final inspection, warranty and payment records together.

Questions homeowners ask

Can a verbal upgrade agreement replace a change order?

Use a signed written change order that records scope, price and timing before the changed work proceeds. A verbal discussion is not a reliable project record.

Does a contractor license bond allow a larger deposit?

Not by itself. CSLB describes a separate qualifying blanket performance and payment bond exception; verify it directly if claimed.
