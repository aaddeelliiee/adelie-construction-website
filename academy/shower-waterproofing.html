Shower Waterproofing: What to Verify Before Tile Goes In

The waterproofing decision happens before you choose the final grout color. A shower needs a planned system that handles water at the floor, walls, corners and drain. Ask to see that system in the scope and verify it while the work is still visible.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Tile is the finish, not the waterproofing plan
  • Review the awkward details before they are covered
  • Schedule the water test as a construction step
  • Compare proposals at the assembly level
  • Keep records that help with future maintenance

Tile is the finish, not the waterproofing plan

Schluter’s technical guidance explains that tile and grout are not inherently waterproof. The assembly behind them needs a suitable moisture-management system. A description such as “tile shower with sealed grout” leaves the essential construction unspecified. Schluter: Waterproofing Membranes for Showers.

Ask the installer to identify the selected assembly and its installation instructions. The scope should name the membrane or liner, substrate, drain connection and treatment of seams and penetrations. A brand name by itself does not explain how those components meet.

Review the awkward details before they are covered

Walk through the proposed bench, niche, curb, doorway and valve openings with the installer. Ask how each is incorporated into the waterproofing assembly and where water drains. Resolve the glass enclosure’s attachment details before someone drills through a finished vulnerable surface.

For a curbless shower, coordinate the floor buildup, drainage and bathroom transition early. Removing a curb from a standard drawing does not establish a workable barrier-free assembly. Likewise, a steam shower needs a design appropriate to steam exposure rather than an assumption that ordinary shower details are sufficient.

Schedule the water test as a construction step

Schluter describes water testing as a check before setting tile and gives system-specific waiting and test instructions. Those instructions are an example for its products, not a universal rule for every waterproofing assembly. Have the installer identify the applicable manufacturer instructions and the local inspection requirements. Schluter: Shower Water Testing.

Include the test in the schedule and keep the result with the project record. If there is a leak or a failed inspection, correct and verify the affected work before covering it. A photograph of a membrane is useful documentation, but it cannot replace the required test or inspection.

Compare proposals at the assembly level

Ask each bidder to describe substrate preparation, waterproofing, drain work, tile setting, sealant joints and any required inspections. Clarify who addresses damaged framing or an unsuitable existing base found after demolition. This is more revealing than comparing tile square-foot prices alone.

In an upstairs condo, include the association’s submission requirements and access arrangements. A repair can affect the unit below, so establish the communication and restoration process before work starts.

Keep records that help with future maintenance

  • Product names and the installation instructions used.
  • Photos of the prepared substrate and completed waterproofing details.
  • Test and inspection records.
  • Approved glass attachment details and fixture locations.
  • Care instructions, warranty terms and the contact for a suspected leak.

After use begins, report persistent dampness, staining or unexplained water loss promptly. Replacing grout is not a diagnosis of a hidden waterproofing problem.

Questions homeowners ask

Can sealing the grout fix a leaking shower?

Do not assume it can. Identify the leak source and the condition of the assembly first. A surface treatment does not establish that the hidden waterproofing and drain connection are sound.

Should the shower be tested before or after tile?

The cited manufacturer procedure places its waterproofing test before tile. The installer should confirm the selected system’s sequence and required inspection so problems can be addressed while the work remains accessible.

Sources and local requirements

References checked October 24, 2026. City of San Diego guidance applies within that city; other cities and unincorporated areas have their own authorities. Product instructions apply to the specified system.
