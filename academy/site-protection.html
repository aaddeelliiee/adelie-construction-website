Construction Process

Remodel Site Protection: Dust, Demolition & Access Planning

A protection plan should show how the household and the construction work can coexist—or identify when they cannot. Before demolition, agree on work zones, access, utility interruptions and the controls appropriate to the materials being disturbed.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Draw separate routes for work and daily life
  • Match dust controls to the activity
  • Resolve hazardous-material questions before disturbance
  • Confirm utilities, retained items and discoveries
  • Define daily handover and reopening

Draw separate routes for work and daily life

Mark the work area, material route, debris route and the spaces that remain occupied. Identify where children and pets will stay, how the crew enters and which bathroom or facilities remain available. If the only route to a bedroom crosses active work, address that conflict before construction starts.

On a small lot, walk the route for deliveries and debris. Check gates, overhead obstructions and storage space. Confirm any required permission for using common areas or public space instead of assuming the curb or neighboring property is available.

Match dust controls to the activity

EPA’s remodeling guidance explains that some dust-producing work needs stronger separation than a simple doorway flap. Ask the contractor to specify the containment and ventilation approach for the actual activity and how it will be maintained. EPA: Indoor Air Quality During Remodeling.

Confirm who checks barriers, protects retained finishes and handles daily cleanup. Discuss the effect on the home’s air systems with the appropriate professional. Do not assume that opening windows or running the existing HVAC will solve every dust or pressure issue.

Resolve hazardous-material questions before disturbance

Ordinary site protection is not a substitute for the procedures required when lead or other hazardous materials may be disturbed. EPA’s lead-safe renovation guidance addresses pre-1978 housing and applicable work practices. Have qualified professionals determine what assessment and controls the project needs before demolition. EPA: Lead-Safe Renovation, Repair and Painting.

Do not casually cut into suspect materials to find out what they are. Keep evaluation, any specialized work and the clearance to resume ordinary construction as explicit steps in the schedule.

Confirm utilities, retained items and discoveries

Before demolition, identify the responsible trade for utility isolation and verify which services must remain available. Mark items to retain, salvage or dispose of. Photograph existing conditions along the work and delivery routes.

Agree on what happens if demolition reveals damage or an unexpected system. The affected work may need to pause while the condition is documented and evaluated. Define who communicates the findings and approves the next scope; avoid making a new installation depend on an unexamined condition.

Define daily handover and reopening

Establish how the work area is secured, where debris is stored and who checks access at the end of the day. Keep emergency routes available and explain any planned interruptions in advance. Revisit the arrangements as the work moves between rooms.

Ask how cleaning and removal of protection will be handled before an area returns to household use. Where specialized controls apply, follow the appropriate professional process rather than using visual tidiness as the only test.

Questions homeowners ask

Can we stay home throughout demolition?

That depends on the work, exposure controls, access and household needs. Evaluate it with the project team before starting; an occupied-house plan should include an alternative if conditions make staying impractical.

Will protective plastic keep all dust out of the house?

Do not rely on that guarantee. Controls need to match the activity and be maintained. Discuss the limitations, cleaning arrangements and any work that requires stronger separation.
