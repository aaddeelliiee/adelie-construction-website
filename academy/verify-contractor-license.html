Contractors & Contracts

Checking a California Contractor: License, Insurance & References

A license number on a proposal is a starting point, not a completed contractor check. Match the business you are hiring to the official record, review its current status and ask how the proposed team will perform your scope. Keep that information with the contract.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Match the proposal to the official record
  • Confirm the scope and the working team
  • Treat insurance as a separate verification
  • Understand what a license bond does
  • Keep a dated hiring record

Match the proposal to the official record

Use the CSLB: Online License Check rather than relying only on a screenshot supplied by the bidder. Compare the business name and license number with the proposal and contract. Review the status and classifications, and read the public information shown for that record. Ask CSLB about anything you do not understand.

If the contract names a different entity from the advertised company, have that explained before signing. A similar business name or a person’s connection to another licensed company does not establish that the contracting entity is the one you checked.

Confirm the scope and the working team

CSLB recommends checking license status and obtaining relevant references. Ask the contractor to explain how its classification and subcontractors relate to the work you want. CSLB: Finding the Right Licensed Contractor.

Request examples of comparable scope and ask references about communication, supervision, changes and completion. A contractor’s experience with a cosmetic kitchen refresh does not by itself establish experience with a structural addition. Find out who will manage the site and who is authorized to agree to changes.

Treat insurance as a separate verification

Request current insurance information applicable to the proposed work and discuss the documents with your own insurer or adviser. Confirm the named entity, dates and relevant coverage rather than accepting “fully insured” as a complete answer. Where verification is needed, ask the issuing agent or insurer to confirm the information.

Review the workers’ compensation information in the CSLB record and ask how the actual workforce is covered. Requirements and exemptions depend on the circumstances; do not infer that a displayed exemption automatically resolves every question about the people who will work at your property.

Understand what a license bond does

CSLB describes the license-bond process and explains how consumers identify the surety associated with a contractor’s bond history. A license bond is not the same thing as a project-specific completion guarantee or an insurance policy covering every possible loss. CSLB: Contractor License Bond Basics.

If a proposal promises additional bonding or coverage, ask for the actual documents and obtain appropriate advice about what they provide. The wording in an advertisement should not be the only evidence.

Keep a dated hiring record

  • The official license record checked before signing.
  • The exact contracting entity and contact information.
  • Insurance documents and any confirmations obtained.
  • References for comparable work.
  • The supervisor, subcontractor arrangement and change-approval process.

Recheck information if the contracting entity changes or the start date moves substantially. Apply the same questions to ADELIE that you would to any other bidder; the proposal should make the responsibilities clear.

Questions homeowners ask

Does an active license guarantee a good project?

No. It is an important verification step, but scope, relevant experience, supervision, contract clarity and communication still matter.

Is a license bond the same as liability insurance?

No. They are different arrangements with different purposes and terms. Ask for the actual documentation and obtain advice about the coverage relevant to your project.
