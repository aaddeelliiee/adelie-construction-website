Whole-Home Remodeling

Remodel Closeout Checklist: Punch List, Permits & Warranties

The last walkthrough should leave you with more than a clean room and a final invoice. Use it to identify unfinished items, understand the new equipment and collect the records needed to maintain the home. Keep construction completion, permit approval and contractual payment obligations distinct.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Walk the project with a written punch list
  • Confirm the permit record
  • Define the cleaning and handover scope
  • Collect warranties and maintenance instructions
  • Reconcile the final account and follow-up work

Walk the project with a written punch list

Inspect each room with the contractor and record incomplete or corrective work using a location, photo, agreed action and responsible person. “Kitchen drawer beside the range rubs when opened” is more actionable than “fix cabinets.” Set a follow-up date for each open item.

Separate corrections from new requests. If you decide at the walkthrough that you want another shelf, document it as a proposed addition rather than silently adding it to the completion list. Keep a record when each agreed item is resolved.

Confirm the permit record

Ask for the required final inspection status and any outstanding conditions. The City of San Diego explains that final inspection follows dependent approvals in its City of San Diego: Types of Inspections. Check the actual project record; a contractor’s final invoice does not establish permit closure.

Retain approved plans, documented changes and relevant test records. If the scope did not require a permit, keep the project’s written scope and any authority clarification that supported that conclusion.

Define the cleaning and handover scope

Review the agreed cleaning work: removal of debris and protection, cabinet interiors, glass, surfaces and remaining construction dust. Identify specialty cleaning separately rather than assuming the ordinary closeout clean includes every surface or existing area of the home.

Ask for a demonstration of new controls, shutoffs, ventilation equipment and service access. Have the installer identify which panels or components need to remain reachable. Record model numbers while they are easy to find.

Collect warranties and maintenance instructions

Build one folder containing contracts, invoices, approved changes, product manuals, warranty terms, serial numbers and service contacts. CSLB advises homeowners to obtain offered warranties in writing. CSLB: Home Improvement Contracts.

Distinguish the installer’s obligations from the manufacturer’s coverage. Ask where a claim starts, what records are needed and which maintenance instructions apply. Save paint formulas, finish selections and any spare material information so later repairs do not rely on guessing.

Reconcile the final account and follow-up work

Compare the final statement with the contract, approved changes, allowance adjustments and payments already made. CSLB’s CSLB: Construction Project Checklist advises documenting payments and avoiding final payment until satisfied with the work. Review the actual agreement and seek appropriate advice if there is a dispute; a checklist does not decide contractual payment rights.

Schedule a later maintenance review based on the products and agreed service arrangements. Check operation, sealants and emerging issues, and report concerns promptly under the applicable terms. A one-year calendar reminder is useful organization, not a promise that every component has a one-year warranty.

Questions homeowners ask

Is the punch list the same as the final inspection?

No. The punch list records agreed construction corrections. The permitting authority’s inspection addresses the permitted work. Keep evidence of both where applicable.

What if a warranty issue appears after the final payment?

Use the written warranty and contract to identify the responsible contact and reporting process. Document the condition and date. Payment does not itself tell you the duration or scope of any warranty.
