Whole-Home Remodeling

Electrical Planning for a Remodel: Loads, Layout & Service Work

An electrical plan starts with what the remodeled home will need to power. New appliances, an addition or future equipment can change that picture. Have a qualified electrician evaluate the existing system and proposed loads before assuming a panel replacement is either necessary or sufficient.

Updated October 24, 2026 · ADELIE Construction · Sources below
In this guide
  • Make an equipment list before discussing capacity
  • Coordinate utility work with construction
  • Place electrical features on the actual room plan
  • Use rough work as a verification point
  • Collect a usable handover

Make an equipment list before discussing capacity

List existing and proposed appliances and equipment with model information where available. Include planned future uses rather than hiding them in a vague request for spare capacity. SDG&E’s service guidance asks customers to work with an electrician or plumber on existing and new appliance loads. SDG&E: Apply for Service.

A count of empty breaker spaces is not a load calculation. Ask the electrician to explain the assessment and identify any information still needed before pricing a service change.

Coordinate utility work with construction

If the project changes service needs or equipment location, establish who handles the utility application and who performs the private-side work. Identify any trenching, access, restoration and inspection dependencies.

Do not tie a guaranteed demolition date to an unconfirmed utility schedule. Keep utility scope and charges visible in the project budget, including any assumptions that have not yet been resolved.

Place electrical features on the actual room plan

Review appliance connections, receptacles, switches and lighting against cabinets, furniture and door swings. Think through where the household charges devices or uses countertop appliances. Have the electrician apply the relevant requirements to the agreed arrangement.

Check controls from the entry routes and intended use positions. Confirm fixture and dimmer compatibility before purchasing a full set of products.

Use rough work as a verification point

Before walls close, review the locations against the current drawings and confirm required testing and inspections. City of San Diego permit guidance covers electrical work as part of its mechanical, electrical and plumbing services; verify the route for the whole remodel. City of San Diego: Mechanical, Electrical and Plumbing Permits.

Do not ask a finish installer to conceal an unresolved inspection item. If the layout changes, document the affected work and determine whether the approved plans need revision.

Collect a usable handover

  • Required approval and inspection records.
  • A clear circuit directory and equipment information.
  • Control instructions and maintenance access.
  • Documentation of planned future capacity or provisions.
  • The contact for faults or follow-up work.

This is planning guidance, not wiring instructions. Leave installation and diagnosis of electrical hazards to qualified professionals.

Questions homeowners ask

Does adding an electric appliance always require a service upgrade?

No. The electrician and utility need to evaluate the existing service and proposed loads. Do not infer the answer from the appliance alone.

Can outlet and switch locations wait until drywall?

That can create rework. Review them against the layout before rough work and confirm them before the walls are closed.

Sources and local requirements

References checked October 24, 2026. City of San Diego guidance applies within that city; other cities and unincorporated areas have their own authorities. Product instructions apply to the specified system.
