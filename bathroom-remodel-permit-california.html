California Bathroom Permit Guide

Do I Need a Permit to Remodel My Bathroom in California?

ADELIE homeowner guideUpdated October 2026

Quick answer

A cosmetic bathroom update may not require a building permit, but relocating or altering plumbing, electrical, mechanical or structural work commonly does. California codes are administered through local building departments, so the final answer depends on the exact work and the city or county with jurisdiction.

Planning guidance for San Diego County homeowners. Costs are illustrative, not bids; verify property-specific scope, prices and permit requirements.

Replacing finishes is different from moving a toilet, adding circuits, changing ventilation or altering a wall. Write the complete scope before asking whether a permit is required; a room label alone is not enough.

A practical way to look at it

Describe the work by trade when checking permit requirements. “Bathroom update” is vague; “move the shower drain, add two circuits, replace the exhaust duct and widen a doorway” gives the building department something it can answer.

Work that commonly requires permits

New or relocated drains and water lines, electrical circuits, lighting in regulated locations, exhaust changes, window or wall alterations and structural work commonly trigger permits or trade permits.

Cosmetic work may be different

Paint, accessories and some like-for-like finish replacements may be exempt, but the related work behind them may not be. Local exemptions and interpretations control.

Why inspections matter

Inspections allow required plumbing, electrical, framing and waterproofing-related work to be reviewed at the appropriate stage. Covered work may need to be reopened if an inspection is missed.

San Diego is not one jurisdiction

City of San Diego, unincorporated county and incorporated cities such as Carlsbad, Oceanside and Vista manage their own permit processes. Use the building department for the property address.

Put responsibility in writing

California CSLB guidance says the home-improvement contract should state who obtains necessary permits. Do not accept an owner-builder role without understanding the responsibilities.

Verify before demolition

ADELIE can help describe the proposed scope and coordinate permit planning. The building department makes the final determination.

Sources and methodology

This guide separates official requirements from planning estimates. Government sources support permit and contractor statements; cost figures are broad market observations, not promises or project quotes. A dependable number still requires a site review and written scope.

Common Questions

What homeowners ask next

Do I need a permit to replace bathroom tile?

Tile replacement alone may be exempt, but changes to waterproofing assemblies, plumbing, electrical or structure may alter the answer.

Does moving a toilet require a permit?

It commonly involves permitted plumbing work; confirm with the local building department.

Who should pull the permit?

The contract should identify responsibility. A licensed contractor commonly obtains permits for contracted work.
