How Much Does a Home Remodel Cost in San Diego?

ADELIE homeowner guideUpdated October 2026

Quick answer

San Diego remodeling costs vary too much for one honest average. A cosmetic refresh may remain below $100 per square foot, coordinated mid-range work is often discussed around $100–$250 per square foot, and structural or high-end whole-home projects can exceed $300 per square foot. Scope, existing conditions and what the price includes matter more than the headline rate.

Cost-per-square-foot can be useful for an early feasibility check, but it becomes misleading when two projects include different rooms, systems and professional services. A kitchen-heavy remodel costs more per square foot than repainting bedrooms; an addition is different from renovating existing space. Use ranges to choose a direction, then price a written scope.

A practical way to look at it

Two proposals can describe the same “whole-home remodel” and be tens of thousands of dollars apart because one includes plans, protection, finish carpentry and paint while the other leaves them out. Before comparing totals, circle every allowance and exclusion.

Three useful planning levels

A refresh keeps the layout and concentrates on finishes. A coordinated renovation replaces major finishes and selected systems while making limited layout changes. A comprehensive remodel may open walls, change structure, replace building systems and rebuild most interior surfaces. Each level needs a different budget and schedule.

Why San Diego projects vary

Coastal exposure, hillside sites, access, home age, jurisdiction, HOA rules, structural conditions and product lead times all affect price. Labor and material markets also change. A number published online cannot account for what is behind your walls or whether the proposal includes design, engineering and permits.

What a complete budget should include

Track construction, cabinets and fixtures, owner-purchased products, design, engineering, permit and inspection costs, temporary housing or storage, and contingency. Confirm sales tax, delivery, protection, cleanup and final touch-up rather than assuming they are included.

How permits affect cost

Permit needs depend on the work and the authority having jurisdiction. Structural, electrical, plumbing and mechanical changes commonly require review or inspection. The City of San Diego directs applicants to check its permit exemptions and project-specific bulletins before applying; other cities in the county use their own processes and fee schedules.

Why estimates can differ

One proposal may include finished plans, supervision, protection and all finish work while another carries allowances or exclusions. Compare the same scope, products, quantities and responsibilities. A lower total is not automatically less expensive if predictable work is missing.

The next step after a range

Define rooms, layout changes, system upgrades, finish expectations and known property issues. Then request a scope-based estimate. ADELIE uses the first conversation to clarify what is included, what is still undecided and which choices have the greatest budget effect.

Sources and methodology

This guide separates official requirements from planning estimates. Government sources support permit and contractor statements; cost figures are broad market observations, not promises or project quotes. A dependable number still requires a site review and written scope.

Common Questions

What homeowners ask next

Is cost per square foot accurate?

It is useful for early planning but not a substitute for a scope-based estimate.

Are permits included in remodeling prices?

Sometimes. Confirm whether permit, plan-check, design and engineering costs are included or separate.

Why do older homes cost more to remodel?

They carry a greater chance of outdated systems, concealed damage, irregular framing and undocumented prior work.
