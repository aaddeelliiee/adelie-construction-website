Rancho Bernardo Additions: Space, Connections & City Plans

An addition in Rancho Bernardo needs to work with both the existing house and the City of San Diego approval process. Begin with the purpose of the room: a larger shared living area, a bedroom, or independent living space can lead to different designs. Decide that before choosing the exterior footprint.

Discuss Your Rancho Bernardo ProjectLocal Planning Resources

Work backward from the room’s daily use

For a bedroom addition, locate the route to a bathroom and consider whether the existing hallway remains private. For a family-room extension, draw seating and circulation before moving the exterior wall. If independent living is the objective, compare an ADU with a conventional addition rather than assuming they are interchangeable.

Use the correct City addition guidance

San Diego’s Information Bulletin 140 addresses certain single-family and duplex additions and remodels within its stated height scope. Its sample drawings are guidance, not construction plans. Ask the designer which submittal requirements apply to the actual building, especially if the proposal has more than one story.

Local reference: San Diego Residential Addition/Remodel: IB 140.

Price the connection, not only the new area

Ask the estimate to separate the new room from alterations needed in the existing home: structural openings, roof transitions, relocated utilities and repaired finishes. Where an association governs exterior changes, submit the intended design through that process as well. Treat association decisions and City approvals as separate milestones.

Decide what can remain usable during construction

An addition built outside the existing wall may allow some work to occur before the connection is opened, but that depends on the plans and construction sequence. Identify the point at which weather protection, utilities or bedroom access will be interrupted. Arrange temporary living around those milestones rather than the total project duration.

Bring these details to the first project conversation

  • A written use for the room and a furniture layout
  • Existing floor and roof elevations at the proposed connection
  • A separate allowance for alterations to the original house
  • City and any association milestones before the building is opened

Use this list to prepare for a discussion with ADELIE. Site conditions and the responsible permitting authority determine the final scope.

Can a standard room-addition drawing be used as my construction plan?

San Diego’s bulletin says its sample drawings are guidance and calls for project-specific plans. The connection to your home and the conditions on your parcel need to be documented.
