Whole-Home Remodel Cost per Square Foot: What It Misses

ADELIE homeowner guideUpdated October 2026

Quick answer

There is no single trustworthy cost-per-square-foot rate for a San Diego whole-home remodel. The room mix, structural work, systems, finish level and excluded soft costs can change the result dramatically. Published local project data is more useful as a broad feasibility check; a room-by-room scope is more reliable for decisions.

Planning guidance for San Diego County homeowners. Costs are illustrative, not bids; verify property-specific scope, prices and permit requirements.

Per-square-foot pricing compresses hundreds of decisions into one number. It works best as a feasibility screen after defining the level of renovation, and worst when used to compare unlike projects.

A practical way to look at it

If someone quotes a square-foot number, ask which square feet they counted and what rooms are included. A 1,000-square-foot scope with a kitchen and two bathrooms is not comparable to 1,000 square feet of bedrooms, flooring and paint.

What the range may represent

Lower planning ranges generally assume finish updates with limited system changes. Mid-range renovation coordinates kitchens, bathrooms and finishes. Comprehensive work may rebuild layouts, structure and systems. Always ask what the reported number includes.

Room mix matters

Kitchens and bathrooms carry more cost than bedrooms and hallways. Two homes of equal size can have very different budgets based on cabinet quantity, tile, plumbing fixtures and appliance packages.

Existing conditions matter

Foundation issues, water damage, older electrical service, galvanized or cast-iron plumbing, asbestos or lead protocols, and undocumented alterations can add investigation and corrective work.

Soft costs and owner costs

Design, engineering, surveys, permits, temporary housing, storage, furniture and owner-purchased products may sit outside a contractor’s square-foot figure. Put them into the total project budget.

Use the number responsibly

Select a scope tier, multiply only for a preliminary range, then test it against room-by-room priorities. Keep a contingency appropriate to the property and do not commit all funds to visible finishes.

Move from benchmark to proposal

ADELIE can review the property, define included areas and systems, identify assumptions and build a scope that contractors can price consistently.

Sources and methodology

This guide separates official requirements from planning estimates. Government sources support permit and contractor statements; cost figures are broad market observations, not promises or project quotes. A dependable number still requires a site review and written scope.

Common Questions

What homeowners ask next

Does square footage include garages or patios?

Not necessarily. Confirm the exact measured area and which exterior or nonconditioned spaces are included.

Is a kitchen remodel included?

Only if the stated scope says so; kitchens can materially raise the average rate.

Does the rate include design and permits?

Often it does not. Ask for an explicit list of inclusions and exclusions.
